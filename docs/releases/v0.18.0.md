# v0.18.0

v0.18.0 lets a channel say how large a message it takes and whether it
takes streams and mail by way of another relay. It also closes what a
second and a third review of the code found. A stream upload is fed
from its first ten seconds or it goes, and opens are counted. A stream
reservation costs its sender and a carried hash its poster. Relays keep
to the minute. A document or a relay's stamp from the future is refused,
and the inbox is ordered by when mail arrived here. Every time that
comes from outside this machine now has a row in the project's limits
ledger stating its bound, or why it needs none. The README now opens
with a note on how the project was made, and
[`docs/PARTICIPANTS.md`](../PARTICIPANTS.md) is new: it says who wrote
what, and under which rules.

The wire level is 2. A v0.18.0 relay takes a version 2 channel
directory, one whose channels carry a rule, and enforces its rules. The
owner's `auu` refuses to push a rule to a relay below level 2, or to one
whose health answer does not say the largest `max_bytes` a channel on it
may name. The relay's schema stays 13. Every new refusal is a status
with a sentence, a code or a `limit=` name, which a client of v0.15.0,
v0.16.0 or v0.17.0 shows as it is. The upgrade itself needs no order,
but the first channel rule waits for the readers of your directory:
see *From v0.15.0, v0.16.0 or v0.17.0* below.

## What changed

### Channel rules

**A channel says what it takes.** `auu channel create` and `auu channel
update` take three rules, each under the relay's own caps and never
looser than them:

- `--max-bytes N`, the largest message the channel takes, in bytes on
  the wire, whether it comes as an envelope or as a sealed stream with
  its manifest. `none` clears it, leaving the relay's own cap.
- `--no-streams` refuses every stream to the channel, and `--streams`
  takes them again.
- `--no-forwards` refuses every message that comes by way of another
  relay, and `--forwards` takes them again.

A directory with any rule is signed as version 2, with `max_bytes`,
`no_streams` and `no_forwards` on its channels. One with none stays
version 1, byte for byte as before.

**The relay enforces them.** A post, a stream's open, every attempt of
an upload and its placement, and a courier's delivery are each judged,
after the route's own cap and after the channel's name and period,
against the directory verified at that moment. Three codes are new:

| Code | Status | Sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `channel-size` | 413 | `channel "logs" takes at most N bytes on the wire; this message is M` |
| `channel-streams` | 403 | `channel "logs" takes no streams` |
| `channel-forwards` | 403 | `channel "logs" takes no mail by way of another relay` |

A stream is refused at its open, before any budget is charged. A
courier's delivery is refused before the carried share is charged, and
comes back to its sender in `auu queue` as refused, with the channel's
sentence. A rule set while an upload is open takes effect at its next
attempt and before its placement.

**The push holds a rule to the relay.** A directory naming a
`max_bytes` over the most the relay takes of any kind, the larger of its
`max_bytes` and its `max_stream_bytes`, is refused `400` naming the
channel, the value and the relay's number. Nothing is stored. The health
answer says that number in a new section, `channel-max-bytes`. Before
any push of a version 2 directory, `auu` asks each selected relay's
health. A relay below level 2 refuses the edit, exit 1:

```
relay work is at wire level 1 and cannot hold a channel rule; update it, or drop --max-bytes, --no-streams and --no-forwards
```

A relay at level 2 or later whose health answer has no
`channel-max-bytes` refuses it too, exit 1, with the advice to update
it. A relay that cannot be asked refuses it, exit 2. A `--max-bytes`
over a relay's number is refused naming the relay and the number. All of
this is before anything is pushed. The pushes still go relay by relay,
so one can take the directory and the next fail; `auu channel ls` shows
which, and `auu channel push` mends it.

**Every listing shows the rules.** `auu channel ls`, and the listing of
a peer's channels, have a `RULE` column, `-` for none, and `--json`
carries the three fields. A sender refused by a rule sees the relay's
sentence, exit 2. The list `auu send` prints for a channel the recipient
does not have gives each channel's rules after its state.

**The first rule says once what it means.** Setting the first rule on a
directory that had none says, on stderr, that peers on v0.17.0 or
earlier will call the directory one that does not verify, in their own
words, until they update `auu`. It says what stops for them, and that
this home cannot go back below v0.18.0 without dropping the rule first.

**A peer's directory this build cannot use is said for what it is.**
`auu channel ls PEER`, the hint after a send to a channel the peer does
not have, and a directory watch tell three cases apart:

- `the directory RELAY served for PEER does not verify against the key
  you hold; do not trust it`, for a signature that does not verify;
- `the directory RELAY served for PEER is one this build cannot read;
  update auu`, for a version newer than this build reads;
- `the directory PEER signed is malformed; tell them`, for anything else
  the peer signed that will not read.

A directory watch drops that one peer with the sentence and runs on for
the others, where it used to end. Your own directory that does not
verify still ends the watch.

### Security fixes at the relay

The fixes the same reviews made in `auu serve` and in `auu` are under
*The expert host* and *The client* below.

**An inline envelope is held to 64 MiB, whatever `max_bytes` says.** A
post, a forward, a courier's delivery and a knock over 64 MiB are
refused `413`, a `knock_max_bytes` set above it included, and a listing
page ends at 64 MiB. A `max_bytes` above 64 MiB still sets a stream's
resume threshold, and the health answer still says it.

**A forward with a path still to go is held to `max_bytes`,** never
above 64 MiB, before the neighbour is looked up and before anything is
queued, written or charged to the carried share, and refused `413`. An
envelope up to 64 KiB over `max_bytes` used to be queued here only for
the neighbour to refuse it.

**An upload is admitted again at every attempt and before its
placement.** The open judged the sender against the recipient's allow
list and the channel, but its attempts did not, so a sender removed
after the open still landed the upload for as long as it kept the pace.
Now every attempt is judged as the open was, before a byte of it is
read, and once more after its last byte and before the placement. A
refusal is answered with the open's own words, and the upload goes with
its partial and its reservation.

**A body refused before its signature is charged.** On every route that
checks a signature, a body refused for its declared length, for its size
or for a read that failed spends a token of the address's
unauthenticated budget, as a failed signature does, and is answered `429`
once that is spent. So a registered fingerprint or an administrator's
can no longer be told apart from a stranger's for free. The admin routes
refuse a body of no declared length with `411`, and one declared over
the cap with `413`, and read the rest whole before the administrator is
looked up.

**The declared length is judged before the identity.** On every route
under `/v1/mailbox/` and on `/v1/forward` and `/v1/forward/failed`, a
body of no declared length is refused `411` and one declared over the
cap `413` before the fingerprint is looked up, so a registered one and a
stranger's get the same answer at the same price; a registered one used
to be told `413` and a stranger's `401`. `auu` always declares a length.
A client of another make that sends a chunked body to these routes is
now refused `411`.

**A courier's mailbox answers as a refusing one everywhere.** Asked for
its channels, the relay answers `403` with the allow list's own
sentence, where it answered `404`, as the post, the stream, the knock
and a forward's delivery already did. A watch that names it among its
peers drops it without a word.

**A failure report survives the hand-over.** A relay woke its onward
worker before it answered a forward, so a fast refusal's report could
reach it before it had recorded the id. The report was answered `404`,
read as final, and the sender was told for good that the mail went on.
Now every answer is written before anything is woken, and a report met
by `404` is asked again on the ordinary backoff until the row's own
lifetime ends, across a restart. A report is bound to the newest row
for its neighbour and id, and one for a row already final is answered
`409`.

**A restarted relay forwards when its queue is next due.** A courier
that finds nothing due now asks the queue when its earliest waiting row
is next to be tried, and sleeps until then, a second at least and an
hour at most. A relay restarted with a retry due in a minute used to
sleep the hour, unless new mail woke it.

**Courier numbers need a peering.** A courier setting, in the file or as
a `--courier-*` flag, with neither `courier.home` nor
`courier.authorized_file` set once the flags are merged in, stops the
relay at its start with `courier sets bounds on a peering this relay
does not have`, ending `give it a peering, or delete the courier block
and the --courier flags`. A home or a file given as `""` or `null` is no
peering, and a flag alone is judged as the file is. Only the presence of
the key used to be checked, so `"home": ""` beside a number started a
relay that forwarded nothing and said nothing. `--print-config` leaves
the `courier` block out on a relay that peers with nobody, so its output
still loads.

**A token dated past the year 9999 is refused at issue.** A token whose
`not_before` or `not_after` is later than 253402300799, the last second
of the year 9999, is refused `400` naming the field, the value and that
bound, and nothing is stored. Such a token used to be stored, and then
the issue answer and every `token ls` failed with a `500`, since no
answer can carry a time past the year 9999. `auu-admin token issue`
now refuses such a date before it signs, with the same sentence; the one
way it could make one was a `--not-after` late on 31 December 9999 with
an offset behind UTC. `auu-ca` issues no tokens. A token whose window
opens later, which the relay has always said it takes, is now added and
listed; adding one failed.

**The kit's update refuses to go on when the relay did not stop.** `sh
install.sh update` stops the relay and then asks systemd whether it is
inactive. If it is still running, the update stops before it backs
anything up or replaces anything, says that nothing was changed, and
names `systemctl status auu-relay` as where to find out why. A unit that
is not there, as when an install is being finished, is let through as
before. The update used to go on whatever the stop did: it copied the
relay's database from under a running relay, replaced the binary and the
unit beneath it, and on a later failure said the relay was stopped.

### Relay limits and charges

**An upload is fed or it goes.** This is the sending rule. An opened
stream upload waits ten seconds for its first attempt, where it waited
the sixty seconds of the resume window. An attempt's deadline moves ten
seconds on only when a whole hunk of 64 KiB has arrived since it last
moved, where every byte used to move it. So a transfer finishes while it
keeps 6.4 KiB a second, and a sender trickling a byte at a time can no
longer hold a recipient's room past its expiry for one open's charge.
A kept partial still lives sixty seconds. A sender may open at most
`limits.max_upload_opens_per_hour` uploads an hour, 64 by default, a
message to several opening one upload for each recipient's copy, set by
`--max-upload-opens-per-hour` too. One over is refused `429` `rate
limited: upload opens; a sender may open 64 uploads an hour on this
relay`, with `Retry-After`, and an open that reserves nothing gives its
count back. The open answer is unchanged. A relay that asks `auu` to
wait, before the first attempt, longer than is left of the ten seconds
less two, has `auu` say so, let the upload go, wait and open it again
once.

**A refused upload is answered before it is closed.** An attempt the
relay refuses part way through its body, a full disk among the causes,
has its answer flushed first, and the rest of the body read and thrown
away for at most two seconds or 1 MiB before the connection closes. The
close used to reset the connection under the sender, so a full disk
could read as a dropped link.

**A reservation nobody fed costs its sender.** A stream upload that goes
holding no byte, aborted, expired, purged or deleted whole, gives back
nothing of its declared size. Holding a mailbox full by opening, sending
nothing and aborting is no longer free. One with kept bytes is given
back its size less them, as before, and an open refused before any
upload exists is given back whole. The relay logs `upload abandoned
unfed`: under `access_log: "full"` about once a minute for each sender,
with its fingerprint, and under `minimal` about one line a minute for
every sender together, naming nobody.

**A refusal for room lets go of expired reservations first.** A post, a
stream open, a knock or a courier's delivery refused `507` for a full
mailbox or a full relay now purges the expired uploads and asks once
more when that let one go. An expired reservation no longer holds a
recipient's room until the hundredth request's purge.

**A carried hash is charged to its poster.** A forward and a courier's
delivery take a hash's cost from a share kept per verified fingerprint:
`courier.carried_rows_per_poster` for a person and
`courier.carried_rows_per_courier` for a neighbour, each with a flag
to match. A hash costs one for each relay its path still names, and a
delivery one. A refusal or a retry costs nothing. A poster over its
share is refused `429` with `Retry-After`, and the access log says
`limit=carried`. Left unset, the shares are a sixteenth and a quarter
of `courier.carried_max_rows`, raised to what the longest path costs and
never above a nonzero ceiling. A share set to 0 is unlimited. A share
set below what the longest path costs, or above the ceiling, stops the
relay at its start, naming the setting.
[`docs/relay.md`](../relay.md) gives the arithmetic.

**The carried ceiling counts the hashes that stand.** `carried_max_rows`
counted every hash, expired ones included, and they were purged only
every hundredth request, so a quiet relay refused a neighbour's delivery
for hashes it no longer needed. Now it counts the hashes whose window
has not passed, and a refusal for it first lets go any that ended and
asks again, on the forward and on the delivery.

### Time

**Relays keep to the minute.** A courier's request is held to within a
minute of its neighbour's clock, once its signature has verified, and
refused `503` with the code `courier-clock` and `Retry-After: 60`,
saying `this relay's clock and yours differ by more than a minute;
relays keep to the minute`. The courier's worker waits it out and says
so once a minute. Past five minutes the request is a `401`, as anyone's
is. Run NTP on a relay's host. Because the chain of clocks is now held,
a sealed stamp, an envelope's, a manifest's or a vouch's, is refused as
from the future only past twice the five minutes a user is held to plus
a minute for each hop the protocol allows: forty-two minutes. It was
five, so two honest peers on one relay, each within five minutes of it,
could differ by ten and one's message was kept under `damaged/`.

**An owner document issued from the future is refused.** An allow list,
a card or a channel directory issued more than five minutes ahead of the
relay's clock is answered `400` with a sentence that begins `by this
relay's clock,` and names `issued`. The five minutes are counted in whole
seconds, as a request's own timestamp is, so a document stamped by a
clock the request was taken from is never refused for the fraction of a
second past it. One issued outside the years 0000 to
9999 is answered `400` with a sentence naming `issued` alone. A
directory is held besides to the years a watch can carry, late 1677 to
early 2262, and a channel's `not_before` or `not_after` to the years
0000 to 9999, each refused `400` by name. `auu` runs the same check
before it sends, `auu channel push` of a stored directory included. A
directory stored under a clock far ahead used to make every later push
from a right clock refused as older. On a v0.18.0 relay such a row is
no bar to the owner's next good push, so a relay that holds one
recovers, once upgraded, without its operator.

**A time a relay sends is held at the client.** A listing's `received`,
a lookup's `created`, a queue row's `last_try`, and the status's
`started`, a mailbox's or block's `created` and a token's `created` and
`last_redeemed` in `auu-admin`'s answers are refused as a malformed
answer naming the field when more than five minutes ahead of this
machine's clock or outside the years 0000 to 9999. A relay's `received`
in the year 9999 in a zone behind UTC used to be kept, and then broke
every later fetch from that relay.

**The inbox is ordered by arrival here.** A record keeps `arrived`, this
machine's clock when it was first saved, through every move and mark.
The inbox, a thread's latest, the completion list and `auu serve`'s
pending messages go by it, then by the relay's numbering. They went by
the relay's time first, so a relay with its clock ahead could pin its
message as the newest, and the latest on its thread. `auu inbox --json`
carries `arrived`. A record an earlier release kept counts as arriving
when the relay received it, unless that time is one this program cannot
write back or is more than five minutes ahead of this machine's clock;
then it counts from when its file was last written, held to the same
two, and otherwise from now, and `auu` warns once per command which it
took. A `received` in the year 9000 used to be taken as the arrival, so
the message listed as the newest for good, and a read wrote that time
back as its own. A `received` of the year 9999 in a zone behind UTC
used to reach `auu serve`'s ledger as the year 10000, which it could not
save, so serve stopped at every start.

### The expert host

**A thread is answered for the peer who opened it.** `auu serve` records
each message's verified sender in its ledger, and a thread belongs to the
first to arrive on it. A message on a thread another peer opened is left
in the inbox unanswered, uncounted, given no worktree or directory, and
said once a run on stderr.

**A thread carried over from v0.17.0 takes its opener from what this
home wrote.** A v0.17.0 ledger kept no senders. A message serve answered
takes its asker from the copy of serve's reply in the sent box. One serve
did not answer, or whose reply's copy is gone, takes its sender from the
message itself while it is in the inbox, and only when it is on that
thread and, once anything on the thread was tried or answered, only when
the earlier release fetched it. A message id is the sender's to choose,
so a co-recipient who planted a note under the id of a message the owner
had removed used to become the opener. Such a note is now said once a
run and opens nothing. Where the earlier release's record cannot say who
came first, the thread is nobody's.

**A carried-over thread with none of its messages left is nobody's.**
Once anything on it was tried or answered, serve answers no message on
it from anyone, the original asker included. Each is left in the inbox,
said once a run, and the peer opens a new thread. The next peer to write
on such a thread used to be answered as its opener.

**A channel written as null is refused by name.** `auu serve` and `auu
serve check` refuse `"channels": {"codex": null}` with `channel "codex":
it is null; a channel is an object, with dir and exec at least`, after
the file's name. They used to stop with a stack trace.

**A worktree git has lost is made again.** What stands at a worktree's
path and is not a worktree of the channel's checkout, one a handler took
`.git` out of or made a repository of its own, is removed and made again
from the checkout, and serve says so on its console. In `side-copy` the
thread's work in it is gone. Serve used to stop on it, and a supervisor
restarted it into the same message.

**A worktree a handler locked or locked out is mended too.** A worktree
git cannot be started in is made again like a lost one, and a
registration the handler locked is unlocked before the prune, and a
directory in it the handler took the permissions off is given back the
owner's and removed. A path that still cannot be removed is moved aside
to `serve/NAME.damaged-N` in the home and said on stderr, to be removed
by hand. One that can be neither removed nor moved aside is the
message's failure in the ledger, retried and given up on like any other,
and the run goes on. Each of these used to end the run with nothing in
the ledger, and a supervisor restarted serve into the same message
forever. Git not installed, or a checkout git cannot answer for, still
ends the run.

**A thread is answered no further than its turns, within a round too.**
Two asks on one thread queued before a round are answered no further
than its `max_turns` allows; the one past them is left unread, as one
past its turns always was. Both used to be answered, and the ledger
counted past the limit.

**An answer and its patch leave together or not at all.** In
`side-copy`, an answer is kept for its retry only once its patch has
been taken, and the patch is kept under the answer's own name. An answer
whose patch cannot be taken is the message's failure, tried again whole
and given up on after `retries`; one whose send failed goes out with its
own patch, never a later turn's. Such an answer used to be kept and sent
on its retry with the thread's latest patch, the turn before's, without
a word.

**An answer serve cannot read back is a failure.** What the handler
printed is read back from the file it was caught in. A file that is
there and empty is still nothing to say. One that is gone or cannot be
read, an after hook's `chmod` among the causes, is now the message's
failure with the reason, tried again and given up on after `retries`,
and so is a kept reply that cannot be read. Such an answer used to be
settled as nothing to say, marked read and lost without a word.

**A stop reaches a fetch in flight.** The fetch that begins each round
of `auu serve` now runs under the round's context, so an expert told to
stop while a relay has not yet answered stops at once, under `--once`
and in the long run alike, with no relay said to be unreachable and no
later relay dialled. It used to wait out the request's own timeout
first.

**A reply is sealed once and posted the same at every retry.** `auu
serve` keeps the envelope a reply was sealed as beside the kept answer,
written to the disk before the first post, and a retry posts it
unchanged. A retry after a post the relay took, its answer lost on the
way back or the ledger not written after it, is one reply under one id:
the relay holds a second copy until the asker's next fetch, which drops
it. Such a retry used to seal the reply afresh, and the asker held two
replies for one question. A reply large enough to go as a stream is
sealed afresh at each attempt, as before.

**A long-running expert retries failed work on a quiet mailbox.** A
message whose handler failed, or whose kept reply could not be sent, is
tried again when it is due, with no new mail to wake the run: an
interval after the first failure, twice that after the second, and so on
up to five minutes between attempts, until `retries` runs out. `auu
serve` used to hold for the next message between rounds with no end, so
a failure on a quiet mailbox waited for unrelated mail or a restart, and
the retries the channel promised went unspent. With nothing to retry the
hold is as it was.

### The client

**The key agent's runtime directory is never a link.** A symbolic link,
or anything not a directory, at `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/auu`, or `/tmp/auu`
with none, is refused by name with the advice to set `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`.
A link another user planted at `/tmp/auu` used to be followed.

**A runtime directory others can reach is refused, not mended.** The
agent's directory with any group or other permission bit is refused,
naming its mode, with two remedies: look at what is in it and make it
0700, or set `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` to a directory of your own. It used to be
made 0700 without a word, which kept whatever another user had put there
while it was open, such as a socket bound to take the passphrase. The
lock file and the agent's log there are opened never through a link and
must be regular files, on Linux and macOS; on Windows the directory under
`%LOCALAPPDATA%` is the user's own and is what is relied on.

**The client asks who answers the agent's socket.** Before sending a
byte, `auu unlock` and every other command that reaches the agent ask
the kernel for the peer's uid (`SO_PEERCRED` on Linux, `LOCAL_PEERCRED`
on macOS), as the agent already asks who called. A socket another user
answers is refused by name and nothing is sent. Windows gives a socket
no peer credentials, and there the runtime directory under
`%LOCALAPPDATA%` is relied on, as the agent already says.

**No connection is kept for a second request.** Go's HTTP transport sent
again, by itself, a request that got no answer on a reused connection,
and a signed request sent again carries its spent nonce, which the relay
refuses `401` as a replay. So a watch whose held listing lost its
connection, to a sleeping machine, an expired NAT entry or a restarted
relay, ended on `unauthorized` with nothing wrong. Now every request has
a connection of its own, and one that dies is that request's failure,
signed afresh if it is asked again. An upload's attempt asks before it
sends its body, with `Expect: 100-continue`, so a relay's refusal is
read before the body goes; a relay that does not answer the ask is sent
the body after a second.

**Every two-step change to the home writes first the step that makes the
other recoverable.** Four places did it the other way round:

- A stream that came again through another relay had its stored files
  removed before its download began. Now the copy is fetched whole
  beside them and takes their place only once verified, and a failure
  leaves the files and the record as they were.
- A stream trusted out of the quarantine had its record published before
  its files were moved. Now the files move first.
- A delivery was saved as acknowledged before the acknowledgement was
  made, so a refused one was never made again and the relay's row stayed
  behind the cursor. Now the record says the relay holds it and that an
  acknowledgement is owed, `ack_owed`, and the next fetch makes it
  without fetching anything again, once it has checked the row is still
  that copy.
- `auu rm --local-only` of a record that will not load removed it before
  the entry that accounts for the relay's copy was saved. Now the entry
  is saved first.

**An interrupted upload is finished on the relay it was opened on.**
`auu send --resume` with `--relay` naming another relay is refused
before anything is opened: `it was opened on work; finish it there with
--relay work, or abandon it and send the message afresh`. Only when this
home no longer has that relay does `--relay` move the upload to another.
It used to open a fresh upload there under a record that still named the
first, so the next resume went back to the first and delivered there.

**A stream is sealed only from the bytes its manifest was signed for.**
Each attachment is hashed as it is sealed and held to its manifest
entry. One overwritten between the two, even with bytes of the same
size, refuses the send before anything is uploaded, saying `FILE changed
while it was being sent; send it again`, with nothing left in the
outbox. Such a file used to be sent, the send said it went, and its
recipient kept it as damaged.

**A fetch is bounded by what the relay held when it began.** A fetch
takes rows up to the relay's last as its first listing gave it, and
leaves the rest for the next fetch. It asks one relay for at most 64
pages, saying once at that cap `this fetch took the 64 pages it asks at
most; the rest waits for the next fetch`. A relay that answered every
listing with one more junk row used to keep a fetch asking without end.
`damaged/` keeps at most 1,024 messages beside its 64 MiB, the oldest
let go first.

**A listing is held to what the relay promised.** A page whose ids do not
rise above the cursor is refused as malformed, naming `id`, with nothing
acknowledged and the cursor where it was, and so is a page with more rows
than the request asked for. A page is held to twice the relay's
`max_bytes` plus 64 KiB and never above 128 MiB and 64 KiB, whatever the
relay says, and a page over 128 MiB and 64 KiB is asked for again with
half as many rows, down to one. A page that declares its length is read into a buffer of that
length and taken once, at about twice its size; it used to cost eight
times. A relay's refusal of a stream download is read to 64 KiB at
most, where it was read whole before each retry.

**A range is answered as asked or not at all.** A stream's chunk must
come back as the span asked for. A relay, or a proxy or cache between,
that ignores the range is a transport failure, and the stream waits on
the relay where it used to be kept as damaged and acknowledged away.

**Every file the home keeps is read strictly.** The serve ledger, a
relay's `relay.json`, `cert.json`, `identity.json`, the outbox's records
and `channel-settings.json` refuse an unknown key, a key twice, a key in
another case or anything after the document, naming the file and leaving
it as found. A misspelt key used to read as absent: a handled entry as
pending, and a channel's misspelt `keep` as off, which acknowledged away
mail its owner meant to keep. A channel in `channel-settings.json` must
say `keep`. Every key v0.15.0 to v0.17.0 wrote is still read.

**`auu quarantine trust` checks what it delivers.** It asks for the
local name before fetching anything. It holds a stream's manifest,
fetched again, to the quarantined sender, id and channel, and refuses a
difference with nothing delivered. A stream whose manifest disagrees
with the size the relay listed is kept under `damaged/` and
acknowledged, at the fetch and at trust alike, where a stranger's used
to be quarantined. A stream whose payload will not open is settled at
trust as the fetch settles it, its manifest kept under `damaged/` and
the stream acknowledged; it used to fail every trust and stay in the
quarantine. Either is settled: the command exits 0 and says on stdout
that the stream was settled as damaged and acknowledged and nobody was
added. The sender is added to the peer list only once the message is
delivered, so a trust that fails adds nobody.

**`auu extract` is all or nothing.** When one attachment fails, the
files this extract already wrote are removed and nothing else is, so a
retry writes the plain names. It used to leave the first files, and a
retry wrote them again beside them under numbered names. A file
somebody replaced meanwhile, or one that could not be removed, is left
and named in the error.

**`auu read --json` and `auu wait --json` say read once they have
marked it.** Each marked the message read in the store and printed the
copy it had loaded before, so the output said `"read": false` of a
message the store held as read.

**`auu outbox drop` drops only what it removes.** A copy whose outbox
record cannot be removed is not dropped: the drop is refused, naming
the file, and the copy still waits and is not marked given up. A copy
whose record went and whose sealed file did not is given up and the file
left behind is named. The removal's errors used to be thrown away, so a
person was told a copy was dropped that the next `auu send --resume`
delivered. Wherever else an outbox entry is cleared, after a copy is
sent or when a send is given up before anything went, a part that will
not go is said with its path, and that the next resume would send it.

**A resume that cannot finish what waits says so in its exit.** `auu
send --resume` now fails when an outbox record it was asked to finish
cannot be resumed, its sealed file gone or not one this program wrote,
counting it with the failures; the whole copies beside it are still
sent. Its warning names the record's file and says to remove it by
hand, since `auu outbox drop` lists only what can be resumed. It used
to warn, leave such records out of the count and exit 0, saying
"nothing to resume" when nothing else waited.

**A section is known only where it is read.** `max-recipients` and
`recipient-count` on a sealed message are held as an unknown section,
kept to 16 KiB and named in `ignored_sections`. A record an earlier
release kept with a larger one still loads. A section marked critical
that this build knows only on another path, a request's name on a
message or a manifest's among them, needs a newer `auu`, as a name never
heard of does. It used to open with the section unread.

**`ignored_sections` names every section its own path passed over.**
`auu read --json` now lists a section a message carried that this build
knows only on another path, the manifest's among them, and a section a
stream's manifest carried that it knows only on another, the message's
among them. Each was ignored on its own path and left out of the list.

## From v0.15.0, v0.16.0 or v0.17.0

There is no order for the upgrade. Upgrade the relays, `auu-admin` and
the clients in any order, one at a time, and send from an upgraded
machine at once. v0.18.0 sends and relays what v0.17.0 does, but for a
channel directory that carries a rule, so the compatibility of v0.16.0
and v0.17.0 with v0.15.0, in [`docs/releases/v0.16.0.md`](v0.16.0.md)
and [`docs/releases/v0.17.0.md`](v0.17.0.md), holds for v0.18.0.

**What waits is the first channel rule.** Upgrade your relays first,
then every peer who watches you or lists your channels, and only then
set a rule. An owner who sets none changes nothing for anyone. A peer on
v0.17.0 or earlier meets a version 2 directory as one its strict decode
refuses, and says, in its own build's words:

```
the directory work served for alice does not verify against the key you hold; do not trust it
```

That is the old build meeting a newer document, not a key problem, and
updating `auu` on that peer is the remedy. Meanwhile its `auu channel
ls` on you and its directory watch on you exit 2 with that sentence.
Its sends to your channels still go through, and a rule's refusal
reaches it as the relay's sentence. Only when a send of its names a
channel you do not have does its hint say the same sentence instead of
naming your channels. A v0.17.0 relay refuses a version 2 directory at
the push, `400` naming the field it does not know, and v0.18.0's `auu`
refuses to push it there first. The container suite's family O runs the
v0.17.0 build itself against each of these, and its rows are what this
section says.

What a mixed pair meets:

- **A v0.18.0 relay and an earlier `auu`.** No `auu` from v0.15.0 on
  sends more than 16 MiB inline, so none meets the 64 MiB cap. An
  earlier `auu` opens an upload and starts it at once, so it meets the
  ten seconds only when it waits before its first attempt; it then meets
  the relay's `410` for an expired upload, and `auu send --resume` opens
  it again. The pace of a hunk every ten seconds and the count of opens
  hold for every sender, each refusal with its sentence. Pushing
  an owner document, an earlier `auu` stamps it with its own clock, and
  a clock more than five minutes ahead of the relay's has the request
  refused as stale before the document is read. An earlier `auu` meets
  the `400` only by pushing a directory it stored while its clock was
  ahead, and it shows the sentence. Asking for a courier's mailbox's
  channels, it gets `403` where it got `404`. An earlier `auu`'s watch is
  woken by any other issue of a peer's directory than the one it holds,
  not only a later one, so a watcher holding a newer issue than the
  relay's is woken once, fetches the relay's and holds that. In
  practice that happens only after a directory issued from the future
  was replaced.
- **A v0.18.0 relay and an earlier `auu-admin`.** `auu-admin` declares
  every body's length, so it meets no `411`, and reads every answer as
  it did.
- **A v0.18.0 `auu-admin` and an earlier relay.** It holds the status's
  `started`, a mailbox's or block's `created` and a token's `created`
  and `last_redeemed` to five minutes ahead of its clock and to the
  years 0000 to 9999, so against a relay with a right clock nothing
  changes.
- **A v0.18.0 `auu` and an earlier relay.** A channel rule is refused
  before the push, by the level check above; a directory with no rule
  pushes as before. A relay with `max_bytes` above 64 MiB fills a page
  to it. v0.18.0 asks again with fewer rows, so it still fetches. A
  single message over the hold is refused by name, and
  [`docs/client.md`](../client.md) gives the two recoveries. A relay's
  time more than five minutes ahead of this machine is refused as
  malformed, but a relay that far ahead already refuses this machine's
  requests as stale, so against a relay with a right clock nothing
  changes. The exception is a relay whose clock was ahead and has been
  put right: it may still hold mail it stamped ahead, and a v0.18.0
  fetch refuses the page holding it until real time catches up with the
  stamp.
- **Between relays.** A v0.17.0 relay still queues a forward up to
  64 KiB over its `max_bytes`. A v0.18.0 neighbour refuses it at
  delivery, `413`, and the sender is told, as before. A v0.17.0 relay
  with `max_bytes` above 64 MiB carrying a larger envelope meets the
  same `413`. A neighbour of either release more than a minute out from
  a v0.18.0 relay's clock has its courier refused `503` `courier-clock`
  until the clocks agree.
- **An expert host with an answer kept and not yet sent.** A reply
  `auu serve` had kept for its retry when it moves to v0.18.0 is
  answered again, the handler running once more for it, since the
  earlier release did not keep the patch it was to go with.

**Going back to v0.17.0.** v0.15.0 to v0.17.0 take v0.18.0 in any order,
and going back is safe for most of what v0.18.0 writes, with the
exceptions after. v0.17.0 reads a record with `arrived` and ignores the
key, and a record it rewrites loses it and counts again as arriving when
the relay received it. A v0.17.0 `auu serve` reads the ledger and drops
the senders, which v0.18.0 takes again as it does from any ledger
v0.17.0 wrote. A relay configuration that sets either new courier share
or `limits.max_upload_opens_per_hour` does not load on v0.17.0, which
refuses a key it does not know, and neither does the output of
`--print-config`, which names `limits.max_upload_opens_per_hour` on every
relay and the two shares on one that peers.

The exceptions:

- **A home that has set a channel rule cannot go back below v0.18.0
  without dropping the rule first.** Its `channels.json` is version 2,
  which v0.17.0 refuses by name, `holds a directory no relay would take
  (v is 2; this build reads 1)`, at every command that reads it, every
  fetch among them. Clear the rules with `auu channel update`, so the
  directory is version 1 again, before going back.
- **A relay holding a version 2 directory cannot go back to v0.17.0
  while it does.** v0.17.0 cannot verify the stored directory, so it
  takes that mailbox's directory as one gone wrong and refuses every
  post to it as having no such channel. The owner drops the rule and
  pushes first.
- **A record saved while an acknowledgement is owed reads on v0.17.0 as
  mail kept on the relay.** A record carries `ack_owed` only between a
  delivery and its acknowledgement, when the acknowledgement was refused
  or cut off. v0.17.0 ignores the key: it lists the message as `on
  relay` and leaves the relay's copy queued until `auu rm` removes it.
  A fetch on v0.18.0 before going back makes the acknowledgement.

**A relay.json saved from v0.17.0's `--print-config` on a relay that
peers with nobody does not load on v0.18.0.** v0.17.0 printed the
`courier` block whatever the settings, with `home` and
`authorized_file` empty and the default numbers, and v0.18.0 refuses
that block at the start:

```
courier sets bounds on a peering this relay does not have: the numbers
under courier bound forwarding, and courier.home or
courier.authorized_file is what makes this relay forward at all; give it
a peering, or delete the courier block and the --courier flags
```

Delete the `courier` block before upgrading. Nothing else in such a file
needs to change.

**A key agent's runtime directory with a loose mode stops `auu`.**
v0.17.0 made such a directory 0700 and used it. v0.18.0 stops every
command that reaches the agent, `auu unlock` among them, with the
sentence:

```
auu: agent: /tmp/auu is mode 0755: the agent's directory is yours alone, and one other users can reach may hold what they put there; look at what is in it and make it 0700 (chmod 0700 /tmp/auu), or set XDG_RUNTIME_DIR to a directory of your own
```

Look at what is in the directory first: a socket or a file you did not
make there is another user's, and the directory is not safe to use.
Otherwise run the `chmod` it names. A directory v0.17.0 already made
0700 is not affected.

**Run NTP on every relay before it peers with a v0.18.0 relay.** A
courier more than a minute out is refused, as *Time* above says.

## From v0.14

Go straight to v0.18.0, in one step. v0.15.0, v0.16.0 and v0.17.0 need
never be installed. The floor is the wire v0.15.0 set, and v0.18.0
speaks it. The order is v0.15.0's, for the reasons
[`docs/releases/v0.15.0.md`](v0.15.0.md) gives, with v0.16.0's one
exception, a large message to several, below. The relay's database is
schema 13 on v0.14 and every release since. Set no channel rule until
every peer who reads your directory is on v0.18.0, as above. v0.11.0
is now retracted in `go.mod` and its tag withdrawn, so `go` names it
as retracted and an install starts from v0.13.0 or later.

**The relay first, with `auu-admin`,** if it is still on v0.14. A
v0.18.0 `auu`, like v0.15.0's, refuses a v0.14 relay's health answer,
which lacks `wire_level`. A v0.14 `auu-admin` misreads a v0.18.0 relay's
answers about one mailbox and a newly added token, as it does v0.15.0's.

**Then each party, in turn, drains its inbox and upgrades.** Drain with
`auu inbox` while still on v0.14, then install v0.18.0.

**A party that has upgraded may send at once,** except a large message
to several to anybody still on v0.14. That includes a large message to a
room with somebody still on v0.14 in it. v0.14 reads a stream's manifest
strictly, so it refuses one that carries a section, as every stream to
several does. It keeps the manifest under `damaged/` as malformed,
acknowledges it, so it is gone from the relay, and says to ask the sender
to send it again. Send that once the recipient has upgraded. Every other
message v0.14 reads as it reads v0.15.0's, and a message to several that
is not a stream reads there as a plain message from the sender.

**A party still on v0.14 holds its sends** to anyone who may have
upgraded, until it has upgraded too. v0.18.0, like v0.15.0, refuses a
message v0.14 sealed as malformed, saying "the sealed record carries no
version".

**A message refused in the window** is not lost. The machine that
refused it keeps it under `damaged/`, and it is sent again once its
sender has upgraded, or, for a large message to several, once its
recipient has. Once upgraded, `auu damaged ls` lists what was kept so.

## Known cases

**An expert on two relays answers a thread for the ask it fetches
first.** `auu serve` fetches its relays one after another. A
co-recipient of a message to several knows its thread, and one who asks
on it through another of the expert's relays before the opener's copy is
fetched is answered as the opener; the opener's own messages on the
thread are then left alone. Nothing of the opener's reaches them. Binding
a thread to its opening message on the wire closes it, in a release of
its own.

**A record kept on the relay with a time this program cannot write
cannot be removed here alone.** A record an earlier release kept on the
relay, with a `received` this program cannot write back, such as the
year 9999 in a zone behind UTC, is refused by its id by `auu rm
--local-only`, a cursor reset and a change of a channel's `keep`. Remove
it with `auu rm MESSAGE-ID`, which removes it from the relay too.

**The inbox shows the relay's time.** The inbox is ordered by arrival
here, but its time column still shows when the relay received each
message, so the two can disagree. `auu inbox --json` carries both.

**A reopened message is the newest.** `auu damaged reopen` saves what it
opens as arriving now, so it lists last in the inbox, as the newest
message there.

**A stream's first attempt that leaves nothing costs its size.** A
sender whose first attempt ends short of the resume threshold, or fails
on the relay's own disk, is charged the declared size for the hour, and
a resend may meet `429` `rate limited: post bytes` with `Retry-After`.

## After this release

Small things found on the way, left for a later release:

- `auu quarantine trust` and a fetch run at the same moment over one
  message whose acknowledgement is owed can leave its record in both
  boxes.
- A copy of an envelope, never opened as an upload, is refused on another
  relay with the upload's words, `it was opened on`; and a copy of a
  message to several moved to another relay keeps the first relay's name
  in the sent record.
- An attachment that grew between its hashing and its sealing is refused
  with a sentence that does not name it, and the sentence for a changed
  attachment begins `stream: malformed:`.
- A message to several whose stream fails while it is sealed says less
  than the other refusals do.
- An upload attempt that stalls and is then refused holds a relay's
  connection up to two seconds longer, for the read after its answer.
- An `auu` whose stream threshold is raised above about 1 MiB may read a
  relay's early refusal of a large inline post or forward as a dropped
  link, since only an upload asks before it sends its body.
- A courier transport of its own, keeping its connections without ever
  sending a request twice, would spare a relay a handshake for each row
  of a backlog.
- The kit's `install.sh update` now refuses to go on when the relay did
  not stop; a kit CI row that injects a failing stop will prove it
  (S55).

Four findings of the third review are scheduled for v0.18.1:

- Two commands changing the sent copy of one message at the same moment:
  the later save can undo the earlier's change; the read, the change and
  the save will be held under one lock (S51).
- An expert's reply above the stream threshold that fails to send is
  sealed afresh on the retry, so a reply whose first attempt did reach
  the relay can arrive twice; the sealed stream and its upload id will
  be kept across the retry (S52).
- `auu serve --json`, run without `--once`, reports a round as written
  when its JSON line could not be written; the write's error will be
  returned (S53).
- One handler output line of 1 MiB or more ends serve's copy of the
  handler's output to its stderr without a word, and every later
  diagnostic is dropped; the long line will be cut at a mark and the
  error said (S54).

CI builds the whole tree on Windows and vets it for Windows from Linux,
and on Windows runs the agent, the lock file, the store, the key formats
and identity, the terminal, the name tests of the regular-file and
channel packages against Windows itself, and the agent end to end and
`auu serve` as second processes; the relay and the rest of the suite
run on Linux and macOS, and the kit and the install script on Linux
under systemd.
