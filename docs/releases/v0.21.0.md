# v0.21.0

v0.21.0 makes `auu serve` answer an ask on a channel it does not serve,
with the channels it does. The wire level stays 2 and the relay's schema
stays 13, so a v0.21.0 party and a v0.20.0 one are the same party, and
everything [`docs/releases/v0.18.0.md`](v0.18.0.md) says about upgrading
holds for v0.21.0 as it is.

**Going back to v0.20.0.** It is safe but for one thing. A serve that has
told an ask which channels it answers on writes an entry under `unserved`
in `serve/ledger.json`, and v0.20.0 reads the ledger strictly and refuses
a key it does not know, so a v0.20.0 serve will not start on it. Remove
those entries from the ledger first; nothing else differs.

## What changed

**An ask on a channel serve does not serve is told which channels it
does.** An ask sent without `--channel` lands on the expert's `default`,
which `serve.json` need not name, and serve left it unread with no word to
anybody, so its sender took the expert for gone. Now a peer's ask on a
channel that neither `serve.json` nor a route of the `handle.json` in the
home names gets one reply on its thread, through the relay it came by,
naming the channels serve answers on, sorted, and with a `handle.json`
the channels its routes take, and saying to send the ask again on one of
them with `--channel` (D178, D179). Nothing of the ask is read and no
handler runs: it is marked read, written in the ledger under `unserved`
with the channel it came on, on no thread, and counts as no turn. The
reply is sealed once and posted again from the same envelope until it is
taken, three attempts at most. `--json` gains a fourth list, `unserved`,
and the console says each in one line. The owner's own asks are left
alone, so agents sharing one mailbox's channels
([`docs/client.md`](../client.md) 1.13) are not told; so is anything not
an ask, a reply to the telling among them, so two serves pointed at each
other send one each way at most; and so is every ask while the
`handle.json` will not read, which is said once. The first start leaves
such asks with the rest of the old mail, and `--backlog` tells them. On
a home serve ran on before, the first round after the upgrade writes the
asks already in the inbox down as skipped and says how many, rather than
telling what may be months of them at once, and marks that it has with
the directory `serve/unserved/`, which a v0.20.0 serve does not read. A
telling whose post failed is let go if its channel is served by the time
it is tried again, or handle's, or its sender is no peer, and the words
it was sealed with are the ones posted again.
[`docs/serve.md`](../serve.md) says which messages are answered.

**A delivery no longer fails as held because another swap's sweep looked
at it.** A streamed message's files are downloaded into a staged copy
held by a lock of its own, and a swap into the same box sweeps the staged
copies there whose locks are free, which a process that died left. A
lock is a file made and then locked, two steps, and a sweep that found the
file in the instant between them held it for that instant, so the
delivery failed at once as held by another process, for nothing; it was
seen on macOS, in a test that runs two swaps at once. The staged copy's
name is eight fresh random bytes, so a lock found held there can only be
a sweep's, and the delivery now takes a fresh name and tries again,
asking as long as it waits for the spool lock and saying held only after
that. The fetch lock is per relay, so two relays fetched at once could
meet here, as could a watch on one and a fetch on another.
